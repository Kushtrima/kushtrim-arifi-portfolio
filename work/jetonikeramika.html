Company

A premium ceramics brand with a 2,500 m² showroom

Jetonikeramika sells premium ceramics and tiles for homes, from bathrooms to living spaces, to private clients and contractors alike. Its centre of gravity is a 2,500 m² showroom where customers see, touch and choose the product in person. The project came through Mardal in 2026, and I took it from research and Figma through to the deployed site.

The problem

A company with a clear philosophy and no way to show it online

Jetonikeramika had no website. Everything had to be built from the start, which meant there was no existing structure to inherit and no precedent for how the brand should look or behave online.

The harder question was what the site was for. A premium ceramics brand could easily become an online catalogue competing on product specs, but that isn't how this company sells. The website had to express the company's philosophy, the brands it carries and the range it offers, in a way that matched the premium experience a customer gets on arrival at the showroom.

Research

The sale happens in the showroom

I researched how ceramics and interior brands present themselves in the Kosovo market, and worked through with the client how their customers actually come to buy.

The conclusion shaped everything that followed. Sales happen through the showroom and through advertising, not online. The website's role is not to close a sale but to tell the company's story well enough that someone wants to visit. That made it a brand site first and a catalogue second.

Solution

Story first, catalogue second, showroom as the destination

The philosophy leads. The first thing a visitor meets is what the company stands for, not a product grid. For a premium brand competing on quality rather than price, that story is the reason to choose it.

The catalogue supports, it doesn't sell. Brands and ranges are presented so visitors can understand the breadth of what's available and arrive at the showroom already knowing what interests them. The final choice is made in person, under real light, with the product in hand.

Every path leads to the showroom. Location, opening hours and how to get there are never far away, because a visit is the outcome the site exists to produce.

Design

Dark, restrained, and built from the brand

The palette comes straight from the identity: a dark background with white type and yellow accents drawn from the logo. Dark gives the brand the premium weight the client wanted and lets product photography stand out against it rather than competing with a bright page. Yellow is used sparingly, for emphasis and actions, so it keeps its strength.

Typography is clean and set with generous space, so the story reads easily against the dark ground. Content is introduced progressively as you scroll, with smooth animations that give the pages a sense of rhythm without getting in the way of reading. Images are large and given room, closer to a printed brand book than a product listing.

Development

A custom build the company can update

Designed in Figma, then built as a custom WordPress theme rather than a page builder, so the team can update ranges, brands and content through structured fields without touching layout. Scroll-triggered animation is written in vanilla JavaScript using the Intersection Observer API, so content is revealed as it enters the viewport without the performance cost of listening to every scroll event. Styling is a single SCSS system of tokens and shared components, so every page inherits the same spacing, type scale and colour logic. Google Analytics 4 was configured from launch to measure how visitors use the site.

Outcome

Five times the monthly traffic, and still growing

Jetonikeramika now has a website that tells its story rather than competing as a catalogue. Visitors understand the company's philosophy, the brands it carries and the range it offers, and every page points them toward the place where the sale actually happens.

Monthly views have grown from around 100 after launch to more than 500, and continue to rise.
