Company

A construction firm with no website at all

HBS Swiss AG runs construction projects across planning, project management and site supervision. The project came through Monun AG, where I work as a product designer and developer.

I had already rebuilt their identity, so the visual language was settled before a single screen existed. The site's job was to carry it into something people could use.

The problem

Starting from nothing, with no reference point

HBS had no website. Not an outdated one, none at all. That removed the usual starting points: nothing to audit, no analytics, no existing structure to argue with, and no evidence of what visitors already struggled to find.

It also meant every decision was permanent in a way a redesign never is. The first version sets what the company looks like online, how it explains its work, and what people expect when they arrive. There was no previous version to blame and nothing to fall back on.

The brief itself was short. The company had to look premium, and it had to explain what it does properly. Both were open questions rather than answers, because HBS coordinates construction projects across planning, management and execution, and that is harder to communicate than a single trade.

Research

Looking at the market, then asking who actually calls

I went through comparable Swiss construction websites, looking at how services are structured, how work is presented, and how quickly a visitor can reach a person. Most fall into one of two shapes: a thin brochure with a phone number and no evidence of work, or a dense corporate site that buries what the company does behind company history.

Neither suited HBS. With no existing site, this one had to prove the company is real, careful and worth calling.

I also went through the company's positioning and service flow with the client: who contacts them, what they ask, and where HBS actually enters a project. The site had to explain the work in a way someone outside the industry would follow.

Solution

Show the work, explain the process, make contact unmissable

Projects get their own place, not a mention. Construction is a trust purchase. With no website and no reviews to point at, completed work is the only argument available, so Projekte sits at the top level rather than inside an About page.

Services are explained rather than listed. Planning, realisation and the stages around them are laid out so a visitor can see where HBS fits into their own project, instead of reading a list of capabilities and working it out themselves.

One persistent action, visually separated. Kontakt is the only filled button in the header, distinct from the navigation beside it. For a business whose entire digital presence starts here, the one thing that must never be more than a click away is reaching a person.

Design

Confident, not decorative

Blue carries the site: strong and saturated, used in full-bleed sections rather than as an accent, with white type on top. It reads as engineering rather than luxury, which is the right register for a company whose promise is that things get built properly. White sections between the blue ones give the eye somewhere to rest and keep long pages from becoming heavy.

Type is a single clean grotesque throughout, set large for headings and plainly for body copy. No display face, no second family. In a sector where credibility comes from precision, restraint in the typography does more than character would.

The interface is built from one repeated form: a softened rectangle. The navigation floats in a rounded bar rather than a full-width strip, buttons are pills, secondary actions are circles, and service panels and the hero share the same corner radius. One shape, one spacing scale, repeated everywhere instead of a new treatment on every section.

Photography got a blue gradient laid over it, which solves a practical problem. Construction images arrive inconsistent, shot on different days in different light by different people. The overlay pulls a varied set into one family, so the site holds together regardless of what gets added later.

Development

A site the company can grow itself

Built as a custom WordPress theme rather than a page builder, so HBS can add projects and update services through structured fields without touching layout. Interaction behaviour is written in vanilla JavaScript with no framework dependency. Styling is a single global system of tokens and shared components rather than per-page CSS, so new project pages inherit the spacing, type scale and colour logic and stay consistent as the portfolio grows.

Outcome

From invisible to findable

Anyone who searches for HBS now finds the company rather than nothing. Visitors reach a clear explanation of what HBS does and where it fits in their project, with completed work as evidence and a way to reach a person from any page. The client adds new projects themselves through structured fields, so the site grows with the company.
