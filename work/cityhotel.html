Company

A four-star hotel that also runs a restaurant

Cityhotel sits in Näfels, in the canton of Glarus. Alongside the hotel it runs Glaré, a restaurant with its own name, its own regulars and its own reasons to visit. The project came through Monun AG in 2026, where I work as a product designer and developer. I took it from the first Figma screen through to the deployed site.

The problem

Two businesses, one website, neither can be a subsection of the other

Hotel guests and restaurant guests are not the same people. Guests book rooms weeks ahead, often from outside Switzerland, comparing rates against other options. Glaré guests are local, deciding within the week, and want a table, a menu and an address.

Building Glaré a separate site would have split a small business across two builds, two hosting bills and two things to keep updated. Folding it into the hotel site as another page would have made a restaurant with its own name look like a hotel amenity. Neither was acceptable to the client.

Research

These are not sites people browse, they are sites people use

Before designing anything I went through how comparable Swiss hotels structure their websites. The pattern that stood out is that almost nobody arrives without a task. They come to check a rate, find an address, look at a menu or make a booking, and everything that is not that task is standing in the way of it.

That set the priority for the whole project. Function carries the site, and atmosphere is carried by photography and spacing rather than by anything the guest has to click through.

Solution

Shared structure, switching identity

The header carries the brand, not the page. Move into the restaurant section and the logo switches from Cityhotel to Glaré, while navigation, layout and behaviour stay identical. Guests keep one mental model of how the site works, and they are told which business they are dealing with in the one place they are always looking. Glaré reads as its own name rather than a page inside a hotel, without a second domain behind it.

Booking a room and reserving a table stay separate. The two transactions carry different urgency, different lead times and different information. One generic Book button would have forced every visitor through a decision they had already made before they arrived, so each path stays visible and distinct, positioned where its audience is already looking.

Nothing gets buried to make room. A hotel carries more pages than a top bar can hold: rooms, restaurant, seminars, events, contact, and everything beneath those. Keeping the header minimal and moving the full structure into a large menu meant the header stays quiet while guests read, and the whole site is one click away when they are ready to move.

Build

Handed over so the hotel can run it themselves

Designed in Figma, then built as a custom WordPress theme rather than a page builder, so the hotel's team can manage seasonal content through structured fields without touching layout. Interaction behaviour is written in vanilla JavaScript with no framework dependency, including the header transition between the two brands. Styling is a single global system of design tokens and shared components rather than per-page CSS, so both identities inherit the same spacing, type scale and colour logic, and new pages stay consistent without additional styling work.

Outcome

One site, two brands, one thing to maintain

Glaré has a distinct identity and its own reservation path without a second website behind it, and the hotel updates both from a single admin. The structure holds as pages are added, which matters for a business that changes its offering with the season.
