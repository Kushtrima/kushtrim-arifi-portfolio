Company

Windows and doors, sold, installed and shown in person

Doratec AG sells and installs PVC windows and doors in Switzerland, with an office and a showroom where customers can see the products for themselves. Its customers range from homeowners renovating to builders and architects working on larger projects. I worked with Doratec directly in 2025.

The problem

A technical product that has to make sense to everyone

Windows and doors are technical, made-to-measure purchases. Each model comes with profiles, materials, glazing and dimensions, and the people buying them range from a homeowner choosing for one house to an architect specifying for a whole building. They want different things from the same product: one asks how it will look, the other asks how it performs.

Doratec had no website at all, so none of this existed online. A potential customer had no way to see the range, compare models or judge the company before picking up the phone.

Research

How people look for windows and doors

I started with the brand, its customers, and the way people search for PVC windows and doors, following the path a buyer takes from a first search to a shortlist of suppliers.

What stood out is how differently people approach the same product. Some start from appearance, browsing for a style that suits their house. Others arrive already knowing what they need and look for the technical detail to confirm it. A single flat product page serves neither well, so product information had to work in layers.

Solution

Appearance on top, specification underneath

Models are presented visually first. A visitor sees the product before reading about it, because for most buyers the first question is how it will look on their house.

Technical detail sits one level down. Profiles and specifications are there for architects and builders, without dominating the page for everyone else.

The company is part of the product. Doratec installs what it sells, which means its people working in your home. Showing who the company is matters as much as showing the windows.

Design

Red, black and white, with precision in the type

Black and white hold the structure of every page, framing the product photography cleanly and keeping technical information easy to read. Red, taken from Doratec's identity, marks the few things that ask for action, so a visitor always knows where the next step is. Montserrat sets the typography, its geometric forms matching a product defined by precise frames and profiles. A translucent header stays in place while scrolling, keeping navigation and contact within reach without covering the content beneath.

Development

Products as structured data

Most of the engineering went into how products are stored. Each window and door model is a structured entry in WordPress with its own fields for profile and technical specifications, and the same data drives both layers of a product page: the visual view that leads, and the technical detail beneath it. A new model goes live with exactly the same structure as every other. The interactive product views are written in vanilla JavaScript and kept deliberately light, so they load quickly on a phone as well as a desktop.

Outcome

The range, explained before the first call

The first phone call to Doratec starts further along. Customers can arrive having already seen the models, compared the profiles and learned who will be installing them, so the conversation begins with what they want, not with an introduction.
