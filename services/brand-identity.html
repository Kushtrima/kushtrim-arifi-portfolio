Logos and brand identity

I draw marks that hold a company in one shape, and build the system that keeps them standing: colour, type, pattern, photography, and a workbook so the identity survives everyone who will use it. Four of them are on this site.

What you get

  • A mark with a reason

    Not decoration. Bären got a bear walking through its own name; Riesen a house cut from the end of a log. The reasoning is something you can repeat to a client.

  • Every version it needs

    Full colour, one colour, the symbol alone, and small enough to sit on the brim of a hard hat.

  • Colour that passes

    Palettes checked for contrast, so white text on your red still reads on signage, in print and on a screen.

  • Type that holds up

    One family, chosen to work from a site banner down to the small print on a form.

  • A pattern and a direction for photographs

    So the brand still looks like itself where the logo does not fit, and the pictures show real work.

  • A workbook

    Colours, typeface, logo versions and the rules in one place, so a printer and a web developer work from the same source instead of guessing.

How it goes

  • Understand

    What the company does, what it is called, and what the name could carry. Often the answer is already in the name.

  • Explore

    Several directions, drawn far enough to be judged rather than imagined. Riesen was chosen from five in a contest.

  • Build the system

    The chosen mark taken through colour, type, pattern and the places it has to live.

  • Hand over

    Files in every format, and the workbook that says how to use them.

Four identities

  • Construction

    HBS Swiss AG — a structural frame, custom letterforms, one blue in four tones

  • Construction and roofing

    Bären Baugruppe AG — a red bear walking through the lettering, four states, roof-tile red

  • Timber

    Riesen Holzbau — a house cut from a single log, chosen from five concepts

  • Education

    WMK — a logo that explains itself, deep purples at AAA contrast
